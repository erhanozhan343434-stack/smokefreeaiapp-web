How to Take a Smoke-Free Break at Work

If a cigarette used to mark the start of your work break, it can help to pick a new place to go and a clear way to end the break. The U.S. National Cancer Institute lists work breaks as one example of a routine that can cue an urge to smoke. Start with the break you most often spent smoking.

Choose a place where you can step away from work

Think about what you wanted from the old break. If you wanted some quiet, try a bench away from the smoking area. If you needed to get away from your screen, take a short walk along an indoor corridor where you’re allowed to go. Choose something that fits the time and space you have.

Give your break a beginning and an end

Before your shift, decide what you’ll do when the break starts. For example, when your 3 p.m. break comes around, fill your water bottle and walk to the lobby. Sit for a minute if there’s room. Head back when it’s time, and write down the first task you’ll pick up. Having an ending makes it easier to take the same break tomorrow.

Make a backup for a crowded or rainy day

If your usual spot is busy or the weather turns, use a second location you’ve already picked. If you only have a few minutes, try a shorter version: refill your bottle, step away from your workstation if you can, and spend a moment somewhere you don’t usually smoke.

Try the same break twice

After two shifts, ask yourself whether the new break gave you the change of scene you wanted. If the timing worked but you still passed the smoking area, try a different route on your next shift. For more examples of routines that can bring on an urge to smoke, see the National Cancer Institute’s guide to smoking triggers.
