How to Drink Your Morning Coffee Without a Cigarette

If you used to light up with your first cup, the smell of coffee or the spot where you drink it can cue an urge to smoke. Try keeping the part you enjoy—the coffee—and changing what happens around it. Start with one change tomorrow morning so you can see whether it fits your day.

Try a three-morning reset

Decide on your first experiment tonight. If you usually drink coffee on the porch where you smoked, leave your mug by the kitchen table. If the timing is the cue, try pouring your first cup after you get dressed instead of right after you wake up.

MorningWhat to tryWhat to notice
1Take the same coffee to a different chair or room.Did the urge start when you poured it or when you sat down?
2Keep the new spot if it helped. If the timing felt more important, try moving coffee until after breakfast.Which change was easiest to repeat?
3Repeat the version that fit best. Put a simple next step after the last sip, such as rinsing your mug.Do you know what you’ll do when the cup is empty?

Give yourself a next step if an urge shows up

Write a plan that starts with “if” and names something you can do right there: “If I reach for a cigarette after my first sip, I’ll put the mug down and step into another room.” If you’re already on your way to work, you could wait to open your travel mug until you arrive and drink it away from the smoking area.

Afterward, jot down the cue and the action you tried: “Porch door opened; I stayed at the table and texted my sister.” One line is enough to help you decide what to repeat or change the next morning.

Keep the routine you can actually use

Once you have a version that works on a regular workday, think about weekends, when your first cup might come at a different time. Pick a weekend version before Saturday. For example, brew the same coffee and take it with breakfast instead of carrying it to your usual smoking spot.

For more ideas on identifying routines linked with smoking and planning around them, see the National Cancer Institute’s guide to smoking triggers and quit plan builder.
