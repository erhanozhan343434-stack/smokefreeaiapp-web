What to Pack for Smoke-Free Errands

Before heading out for errands, put a few useful items in the bag you already carry. Think about the gaps between stops: waiting for an order, standing outside a store, or sitting at a bus stop. Choose something you can do in those moments when you would usually reach for a cigarette.

Start with a small, practical list

The National Cancer Institute’s Smokefree.gov suggests planning activities for cravings in advance. Its craving-management guide includes drinking water, chewing gum, and contacting someone supportive. Use those ideas to choose a few things that fit your outing.

  • A water bottle. Fill it before leaving and keep it easy to reach.
  • Ordinary chewing gum. Pick a flavor you enjoy if gum suits you.
  • A short list of distractions. Write down a puzzle, a saved article, or another quiet activity you can use while waiting.
  • A support contact. Save the name of someone you can text, along with a brief message you could send.

Match the bag to your stops

Consider what you can comfortably carry and use. A puzzle on your phone may suit a bus ride. A small notebook may be easier when you’re waiting without a reliable connection. Put your chosen items in one pocket so you can find them without emptying the bag.

Keep the list short. If you won’t use an item on this trip, leave room for what you actually need. Check that your phone is charged if your plan depends on it.

Walk through one example

Suppose you’re taking the bus to pick up groceries and a prescription. Before leaving, pack your water and gum, save a crossword, and choose a friend to text. A message might say, “I’m waiting for my order and want a cigarette. Can you chat for a few minutes?” Ask your friend beforehand whether they’re available, and choose another activity for times they can’t reply.

If a wait turns out longer than expected, open the saved puzzle or read the note you brought. Choose an activity that fits the place you’re in and keeps your attention on your surroundings.

Reset the bag when you get home

Refill the water bottle, replace what you used, and note one thing to adjust for next time. Perhaps the notebook was easier than your phone, or you wanted the gum in an outside pocket. Use that observation to prepare for your next trip.

For more options, see the NCI preparation guide and craving-management guide. Smokefree.gov is a U.S. government resource, separate from the SmokeFree AI app.
