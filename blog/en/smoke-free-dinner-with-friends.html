How to Plan a Smoke-Free Dinner With Friends

When you’re meeting friends for dinner after quitting smoking, plan for the moment someone says, “I’m going outside.” Decide where you’ll sit, what you’ll say, and what you’ll do while they’re gone. You can make those choices before anyone orders.

Pick your seat before you get there

If the group usually eats on a patio where people smoke, ask for an indoor table or suggest a restaurant with a smoke-free patio. If someone else booked the reservation, text the host early: “Could we sit inside? I’m taking a break from being around cigarettes.”

Have one reply ready for the smoking break

Try a line that sounds like you: “I’m staying in. I’ll see you when you get back.” If someone offers you a cigarette, a simple “No thanks, I quit” is enough. Pick something you can do at the table, like asking for the dessert menu or starting a conversation with someone who stayed inside.

Make a backup plan for after dinner

The group might decide to move to a smoking patio after the check comes. Before dinner, pick a nearby place where you’d be comfortable continuing the evening. You could say, “I’m going to grab ice cream around the corner if anyone wants to join.” If you’re ready to head home, make sure you have a way to leave when you want to.

Put the plan in one note

  • Seat: Ask for an indoor booth when I arrive.
  • When friends step out: Stay inside and ask Maya about her weekend trip.
  • After the check: Suggest the ice cream shop if the group wants to keep hanging out.

Afterward, note which part was easy and which caught you off guard. If the hardest moment was waiting by the entrance for your ride, choose a different pickup spot next time. The U.S. National Cancer Institute’s guide to smoking triggers explains why social settings can prompt an urge and how to plan around them.
